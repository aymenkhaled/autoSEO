✅ The audit is correct — here's why
I checked client/index.html and the page components. The findings are accurate:

1. "Title Too Long" — REAL ❌
Your <title> in client/index.html line 9:

"StrategyNavigator.ai - AI-Powered Strategic Planning Platform for Business Leaders" = 85 characters

Google truncates titles at ~60 chars (~580px). Yours gets cut to:

"StrategyNavigator.ai - AI-Powered Strategic Planning Platf…"

Worse — every single page on your site uses the exact same title. You have no per-route title management (no react-helmet-async, no <Head> component anywhere). So /pricing, /about, /faq, /login — all serve the identical 85-char title. That's why the audit shows it 17 times.

2. "Missing H1 Tag" on 20 pages — TECHNICALLY REAL ❌
Your code DOES have <h1> tags in 30+ page components (landing, pricing, about, etc.) — but here's the catch:

Your app is a client-side rendered React SPA. When any SEO crawler (Apollo, Ahrefs, SEMrush, Screaming Frog, Google's first pass) fetches your URL, the raw HTML is just:

<body>
  <div id="root"></div>   ← That's it. No H1. No content.
</body>

The H1 only appears after React downloads, parses, and renders. Google's modern crawler does eventually run JS, but:

It's slower (days vs minutes for indexing)
Some pages get budget-cut and never JS-rendered
Most SEO audit tools don't run JS at all
So Apollo sees: no H1 anywhere on your site. Correct, from a crawler's perspective.

3. "Thin Content (10 words)" — SAME ROOT CAUSE ❌
Same issue. The crawler sees ~10 words of visible body text (essentially nothing) because React hasn't rendered yet. Your actual landing page has thousands of words — but they're invisible to the crawler.

You have no SEO rendering strategy for a React SPA marketing surface. Every fix flows from this.

🛠 The fix — 3 steps, ranked by ROI
Step 1 — Shorten the title and add per-route titles (1 day, fixes 17/80 issues immediately)
Install react-helmet-async and add per-route <Helmet> components to your public pages. Suggested titles (all ≤60 chars):

Page	New Title
/ (landing)	AI Strategic Planning for Business Leaders | StrategyNavigator (62)
/pricing	Pricing — Strategic Planning Software | StrategyNavigator (57)
/about	About StrategyNavigator.ai — Our Mission (40)
/faq	FAQ — StrategyNavigator.ai (26)
/contact	Contact Sales | StrategyNavigator.ai (36)
/privacy-policy	Privacy Policy | StrategyNavigator.ai (36)
/terms-of-service	Terms of Service | StrategyNavigator.ai (38)
Each <Helmet> should also set its own <meta name="description"> (140–160 chars).

Step 2 — Pre-render your public pages (1 day, fixes 37/80 issues)
You don't need to switch to Next.js. Easiest path:

Option A — vite-plugin-prerender or react-snap (recommended)
Builds your SPA, then runs a headless browser over your public routes (/, /pricing, /about, /faq, /contact, /privacy-policy, /terms-of-service) and writes static HTML files. Crawlers get fully-rendered HTML with H1s and full content. Works with your existing Vite + React + wouter setup. No code rewrite.

Option B — Server-render marketing pages only (more work)
Have Express render the public landing/pricing/about HTML server-side, while keeping the authenticated app as SPA.

After this: your H1s, content, and per-route titles all become visible to crawlers.

Step 3 — Add sitemap.xml and robots.txt (1 hour)
I didn't see either in client/public/. Without them, Google has to discover pages by guessing. Generate:

sitemap.xml listing all your public marketing routes with <lastmod> dates
robots.txt allowing public pages, disallowing /admin, /api, /dashboard, /settings, /profile
Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console (your verification token is already in index.html line 20 — good).
